## Planning before you adopt Notion

Before moving important work into a new workspace, decide what you need to organise. Examples include project briefs, meeting notes, personal tasks, reference material, and team documentation.

Start with one small workspace and a simple structure. Add databases, templates, or linked pages only when they solve a real problem. Too many properties and dashboards can make a workspace harder to maintain.

## Questions to check before choosing a plan

- Which collaboration and sharing controls are available on the plan you need?
- Are file uploads, version history, automation, or other limits relevant to your workflow?
- Do the integrations you depend on work with your other services?
- What happens to your content and access if your subscription or team membership changes?
- Does your organisation have privacy, retention, or access-control requirements?

Features and limits can change. Confirm the current details on the [official Notion website](https://www.notion.so/) before committing.

## Advantages and trade-offs

A combined workspace can reduce the need to switch between separate notes, documents, and project pages. However, a flexible system requires consistent organisation. Teams should agree on naming, ownership, permissions, and how information is maintained.

## A practical first setup

1. Create a home page for the work you actually do.
2. Add one task list or project database if needed.
3. Create a reusable meeting-note or project template.
4. Test sharing and permissions before inviting others.
5. Review the structure after using it for a week.

Avoid storing confidential information until you have reviewed the service's current security controls and your own organisation's requirements.
