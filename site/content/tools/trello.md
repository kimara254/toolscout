## What is Trello?

A visual project management tool built around boards, lists, and cards.

## Key features


- Visual task boards

- Track work through lists and cards

- Organize personal or team projects


## Who is it for?

Individuals and teams who prefer a visual overview of tasks.



## Where to learn more

Visit the official website to check the current features, plans, and terms before deciding whether the tool suits your needs.

## How Trello boards work

Trello organises work into boards, lists, and cards. A board represents a project or workflow, lists represent stages, and cards represent individual tasks or items.

For example, a small content project could use lists such as Ideas, Planned, In progress, Review, and Done. Move cards between stages as the work advances.

## Features to evaluate

- Assigning work and setting due dates.
- Checklists, attachments, labels, and comments.
- Automation options and their plan limits.
- Calendar or other views available to your team.
- Integrations with the tools your workflow already uses.
- Guest access, permissions, and administrative controls.

Check the [official Trello website](https://trello.com/) for current features, plan limits, and pricing.

## A practical setup

1. Create one board for a clearly defined project.
2. Use a small number of lists that reflect your actual workflow.
3. Give each card a clear outcome and, where appropriate, an owner.
4. Add due dates only when there is a genuine deadline.
5. Review the board regularly and archive completed work.

## Advantages and trade-offs

The visual layout makes task status easy to understand, especially for straightforward workflows. Complex projects may need additional structure, reporting, or integrations. Before choosing a plan, check whether the features and collaboration controls you need are included.

A board is useful only when the people using it agree on how cards are created, updated, and completed.
